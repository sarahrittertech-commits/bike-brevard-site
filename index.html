Who it's for

Families first.
Riders and visitors too.

01

Riding with kids?

Short routes with a playground or an ice cream stop built in, so little riders get a win before they get tired.

In the pickup line at school, other moms told me how hard it is to juggle elementary and middle school pickups. A quick look at the bike path shows the playgrounds along the way, where families can meet up after school.

Featured ride Playground to Dolly's, "The after-school classic"

02

Here to ride?

Brevard's bike shops (The Hub, Squatch, Sycamore Cycles and others) let you demo a bike, especially if you're thinking of buying. Take it from the shop to the path and straight out to Pisgah.

That's a real test ride, with no bike rack and no trailhead parking.

Featured rides Gateway to Pisgah; The Hub to Bracken and Back

03

Here for the breweries?

Leave the car at the hotel. The Taproom Traverse strings the taprooms in town together on the bike path.

Featured ride The Taproom Traverse

About me

Riding the path with Em

I'm Sarah Ritter, a Brevard mom who has been riding the bike path with my daughter Em since she was eight months old.

Brevard is a little town in Appalachia known for its mountain biking. I wasn't a biker when I moved here. Em was inspired as a baby, watching the local college girls' team ride, and I've been trying to keep up with her ever since.

  1. 8 months The trailer

    It started with a cheap bike off Facebook Marketplace and a trailer behind it.

  2. 18 months The pump track

    I had a babysitter for the afternoon. Em tried the local pump track and decided she loved biking. I've been trying to keep up ever since.

  3. 20 months The shotgun seat

    I moved her up to a Kids Ride Shotgun seat, upgraded to a mountain bike, and we hit the trails. With my sidekick up front yelling "Wahoo!" and "Faster, mommy!", I found the confidence for little bumps, our first rollers, and rides through creeks and streams.

  4. Strider On her own wheels

    She raced BMX on a Strider. Once I had to sprint after her as she took off downhill toward a brewery half a mile from our house, because she wanted french fries.

  5. Pedal bikes Pedalling

    She started on a 12" bike, then a 14" kids mountain bike, and we were back on the trails at DuPont. Then came a 16", and now a 20" with gears. Most of her bikes were secondhand, traded and swapped with friends around town.

  6. Age 4 Riding across town on her own

    The bike path made it possible for Em to ride safely across Brevard herself. Now she's elementary-aged and rides to school.

Every day

How we use the path

We've ridden it since Em was a baby, often as our daily commute when we didn't have other reliable transportation.

  • Preschool drop-offs, and now riding to school
  • Rides to the forest to go swimming, and to the town pool
  • Playground meet-ups with friends, then ice cream
  • Skipping the parking hassle at the Christmas and Halloween parades
  • Riding to community events around town
  • Catching some live music at local breweries
  • Dropping our bikes off for repair
  • Rescuing ourselves: a quick ride to the auto parts store for jumper cables when the car battery died, and when we had a flat
